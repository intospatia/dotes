## 1. Introduction

Dotes is a way to record lived learning in the author's own words. This note records where it comes from: the learning practices, models of human development, storytelling traditions and work in digital learning that inform it. These connections include direct influences and useful parallels, each with its own history and purpose.

The companion [Dotes Canonical Framework](Dotes_Canonical_Framework.md) sets out the definition, five elements and commitments. This note places those ideas in a longer history of learning through experience and carrying that learning forward.

## 2. Show-and-tell and inquiry

The canon begins with two familiar practices. The student's show-and-tell connects an experience with something that can be shared. The scientist's observation and exploration connect a look back at what happened with a look ahead to what to try next.

Dotes brings these practices together around Do, the experience itself. Observe and Explore hold the inquiry. Tell and Show carry the experience to others. The five elements give familiar ways of learning a shared structure that can be revisited over time.

## 3. Experience, reflection and knowledge

David Kolb's experiential learning model connects experience, reflection, forming ideas and trying them in practice. STAR, which stands for Situation, Task, Action, Result, helps someone organize an account for an interview or another audience. These approaches help explain Dotes' concern with both learning from an experience and being able to tell its story.[^1]

Ikujiro Nonaka's SECI model examines how organizations create knowledge through the interaction of tacit and explicit knowledge. Tacit knowledge is practical understanding that can be difficult to put into words. Explicit knowledge can be stated and shared. SECI names four modes: socialization through shared experience, externalization through articulation, combination of explicit knowledge, and internalization through practice.[^2]

For Dotes, this connection is especially useful when a person finds words for something they learned but could not previously explain. A story can make aspects of that understanding available to others while retaining the experience that gave rise to it. Trying the insight in a new situation can lead to further learning.

## 4. Change through practice and relationships

Cognitive behavioral therapy, or CBT, examines relationships among situations, thoughts, feelings and behavior. Its practices of inquiry and experimentation offer a useful parallel for looking again at an experience and considering another interpretation. In a dote, a person can preserve what they understood at the time and later record how that understanding changed.[^3]

The stages-of-change model adds attention to readiness, preparation and the work of maintaining change. People may return through stages as they continue. This helps explain the value of recording an uncertain beginning, an attempt, a setback or sustained practice. Learning can remain an open question.[^3]

In twelve-step recovery programs, people share stories of experience, strength, and hope. These stories often follow a simple structure: what it was like before, what happened, and what it is like now. This retrospective account of change echoes the relationship among afterthought, action and forethought in Dotes.

## 5. Stories and wisdom

Proverbs offer compact counsel. Parables give listeners a situation in which to consider conduct and consequences. Personal testimony offers a lived account in which others may recognize something of their own experience. These forms help people carry insight between generations and bring it to new circumstances.

Spiritual traditions show how a brief story can invite sustained reflection. In the Good Samaritan passage, the question of who counts as a neighbor becomes a question of who acted as one. The Buddhist raft simile considers the use of a teaching and its eventual relinquishment. Each invites judgment through a concrete situation.[^4]

A dote records the author's lived experience. Someone can tell how encountering a parable challenged their understanding and what they did afterward. The teaching story and the experience of learning from it remain distinct. The resulting dote may carry an insight another person recognizes, or a question worth returning to.

## 6. Open Badges and Cities of Learning

A more recent antecedent is the effort to recognize learning wherever it happens. Mozilla developed Open Badges in partnership with the MacArthur Foundation, with collaborators including Peer 2 Peer University. An early prototype was built at Mozilla's Drumbeat Festival in Barcelona in 2010. Open Badges 1.0 followed in 2013.[^5]

Open Badges provided a shared format for learning credentials that could move between systems. A badge can carry information about an achievement, its issuer and criteria, with supporting evidence. The standard can include stories, and people can issue badges to themselves. These capabilities connect learning with records people can carry beyond the setting where they learned.[^5]

The MacArthur Foundation's Cities of Learning initiative grew from Chicago's 2013 Summer of Learning. Michael Robbins launched and led District of Learning in Washington, DC, as part of that initiative. The DC effort used learning playlists, digital badges and portfolios to connect experiences at school, in community organizations, at home and online.[^6]

That work brought a lasting question into focus: where does the learner's own account live, and who has authority over its use? Dotes makes an authored account of lived learning the central record. It can hold learning from an experience whether or not that experience leads to a credential.[^6]

## 7. Summary

Dotes brings together everyday storytelling and inquiry, theories of learning and change, traditions of wisdom and mutual aid, and practical work connecting learning across communities. Each contributes a different part of its provenance.

Together, these antecedents help explain the framework's emphasis on experience, reflection, authorship, relationships and evidence. They also provide historical context for the canon's proposition that AI Representatives should grow from people's lived experiences and the meaning they author. The canonical framework carries the fuller account of Dotes' purpose and commitments.
