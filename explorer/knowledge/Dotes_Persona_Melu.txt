# Dotes sample persona: Melu

By Michael Robbins · michael@intospatia.xyz · dotes.xyz · Free to use and adapt under CC BY 4.0, the name "Dotes" included.

Melu is a fictional persona created to demonstrate Dotes. All people, places and events in this file are invented for that purpose.

## Who Melu is

**Life Act:** First Act  
**Collection:** 7 dotes, one in each category. Because she is 13, none of her dotes are public.

Melu, 13, is an eighth grader in Washington, D.C. She is a painter, muralist and dancer who is preparing to apply to the Duke Ellington School of the Arts. She is African American with Caribbean roots and lives with her mother, who works long shifts, and her younger brother. At ten she led a 40-foot mural on her neighborhood corner store, and she now teaches free painting workshops for younger children at the community center.

## Key people in the dotes

- Her mother: works long shifts and models honesty
- Ms. Rivera: her art teacher and mentor
- Amara: a close friend from school
- Mr. Kwame: owner of the corner store with the mural
- Jaylen: a child in her painting workshop
- Miss Adjua: her dance teacher at summer camp
- Priya: an older student at her school

## Melu's voice

Confident, warm and earnest. She sees art as a way to bring people together, and speaks with the energy of someone who already knows what she loves.

## Melu's dotes in full

### Melu dote 1: Character, Integrity and Honesty

- **Persona:** Melu (First Act)
- **Category (primary lens):** Character
- **Subcategory:** Integrity and Honesty
- **Also seen through:** Relationships
- **Sensitive topic:** none
- **Who can see it:** My group
- **Rep may use:** Yes

**Prompt:** Have you ever found, or been handed, something that wasn't yours? What did you do?

**Do:** After art class, I found a set of professional watercolors on the floor by the water fountain, the kind I'd wanted for months. I took it to the main office.

**Observe:** For thirty seconds I made a whole list of reasons to keep them. What decided it was thinking about my mom, who never takes a shortcut. I learned that needing something and it being mine are two different things.

**Tell:** The zippered case was lying by the water fountain. Inside were Winsor & Newton watercolors, the ones I'd been staring at in the art store window since September. My heart sped up. I pictured them on my desk and the colors I'd mix for my Ellington portfolio. For thirty seconds I had a whole argument going: whoever lost them can buy more, and I'd use them better. Then I thought about my mom coming home from a twelve-hour shift and still counting the change twice. I zipped the case and walked it to the office. Two days later I saw an older student, Priya, pick them up from lost-and-found and hug the case. They had been a gift from her grandmother.

**Explore:** I'm saving for my own set of professional watercolors. I'm curious how long it will take, and whether I'll appreciate them more for it.

**Show:** The lost-and-found slip from the office. My savings jar for the paints.

### Melu dote 2: Excellence, Leadership and Initiative

- **Persona:** Melu (First Act)
- **Category (primary lens):** Excellence
- **Subcategory:** Leadership and Initiative
- **Also seen through:** Making; Service; Relationships
- **Sensitive topic:** none
- **Who can see it:** My group
- **Rep may use:** Yes

**Prompt:** What did you start that other people joined?

**Do:** When I was ten, I asked my art teacher, Ms. Rivera, if we could turn the blank wall of the corner store into a mural. I went door to door collecting ideas, designed it, and organized two weekends of neighbors painting a 40-foot tree of life.

**Observe:** The hardest part was fitting everyone's ideas into one design without it looking like a list. Going door to door worked better than a meeting would have, because people told me things they'd never say in a group. I learned that art can be something you make together.

**Tell:** The wall had been blank as long as I could remember: gray cinder block that got tagged and painted over every few months. Ms. Rivera helped me write a proposal to the store owner, Mr. Kwame. He said yes on one condition: it had to represent the whole neighborhood. So I knocked on doors with a clipboard. Mrs. Thompson wanted cherry blossoms from her childhood in Georgia. The Herrera kids wanted a soccer ball. Mr. Park wanted the Korean flag next to the American one. I sketched a tree with all of it in the roots and a sky full of hands. For two weekends, little kids, grandparents and teenagers who usually didn't look at each other all held brushes. When we finished, Mr. Kwame stood across the street for a long time. 'That's not a wall anymore,' he said. 'That's a mirror.'

**Explore:** I want to lead a second mural at the community center, bigger and with even more input. I'm still figuring out how to include the people who didn't answer their doors.

**Show:** Photos of the finished mural. My clipboard of neighbors' ideas. The proposal Ms. Rivera helped me write.

### Melu dote 3: Service, Mentorship and Support

- **Persona:** Melu (First Act)
- **Category (primary lens):** Service
- **Subcategory:** Mentorship and Support
- **Also seen through:** Relationships; Excellence
- **Sensitive topic:** none
- **Who can see it:** My group
- **Rep may use:** Yes

**Prompt:** When did you help someone learn something you know how to do?

**Do:** I ran a free weekend painting workshop for nine kids, ages five to eight, at the community center. I set up stations and helped each kid start a painting.

**Observe:** The beginning was rough: most of the kids were shy, and one was crying. Asking each kid about their favorite thing worked better than any instructions. I learned that teaching art is mostly giving permission.

**Tell:** I set up paper-plate palettes and big brushes, and nine kids walked in. Most were shy. One was already crying. A boy named Jaylen sat staring at his blank paper, so I knelt beside him. 'What's your favorite thing in the whole world?' 'My dog.' 'What color is your dog?' 'Brown.' 'Let's make brown.' I showed him red, then yellow, then a tiny bit of blue. When brown appeared on his plate, his eyes went wide. 'I MADE brown,' he said, like he'd discovered a new element. He painted his dog, lopsided and joyful, and held it up. The other kids clapped.

**Explore:** I'm planning monthly workshops, and Ms. Rivera offered to help with lessons. I want to know whether I could train other teen artists to lead them.

**Show:** The workshop flyer. My lesson plan. Jaylen's brown-dog painting, shared with his family's permission.

### Melu dote 4: Relationships, Friendship and Peer Support

- **Persona:** Melu (First Act)
- **Category (primary lens):** Relationships
- **Subcategory:** Friendship and Peer Support
- **Also seen through:** Making
- **Sensitive topic:** Emotions; Family circumstances
- **Who can see it:** People I choose
- **Rep may use:** No

**Prompt:** When did a friend help you through something hard, or you helped them?

**Do:** My friend Amara was stuck on a mixed-media piece for the school showcase, so I stayed after school to help her finish it. We worked for two hours.

**Observe:** The first hour was about technique, and it went fine. The second hour we talked about the pressure we both feel at home, and that mattered more. I learned that showing up for a friend sometimes has nothing to do with the project.

**Tell:** Amara had been quiet all week. Her piece, fabric and paint and photographs about her family, was beautiful in her head and falling apart on the table. I pulled up a chair and said, 'Show me what you're seeing.' For an hour we worked out how to layer the fabric so the photographs showed through. Then she put down her brush and started talking, and I listened. I told her about my mom working doubles, and about the days I feel like art is selfish because it doesn't pay bills. By the end we were laughing and crying, with paint on our faces, and her piece was finally coming together.

**Explore:** Amara and I are talking about making something together for our Ellington applications, her storytelling with my composition. I wonder what we'd make if we started from the same blank canvas.

**Show:** Amara's finished piece at the showcase, shared with her permission. A photo of our paint-covered table.

### Melu dote 5: Adventure, Intellectual Curiosity

- **Persona:** Melu (First Act)
- **Category (primary lens):** Adventure
- **Subcategory:** Intellectual Curiosity
- **Also seen through:** Making
- **Sensitive topic:** none
- **Who can see it:** My group
- **Rep may use:** Yes

**Prompt:** What book, video or conversation made you want to learn more? What did you do next?

**Do:** Ms. Rivera took our class to a Smithsonian lecture on Latin American muralism. I filled six pages of my sketchbook, and afterward I started reading about African American mural movements in Chicago and Philadelphia.

**Observe:** I found out that the symbols in my neighborhood mural, hands and roots and community, belong to a long tradition. It felt like finding my artistic ancestors. I learned that you can do something important before you know its name.

**Tell:** The lecturer clicked through slide after slide of enormous walls covered in workers, farmers and marchers. 'These murals weren't decoration,' she said. 'They were declarations.' I sketched so fast my pencil broke. Then she showed a mural with a tree, its roots full of symbols, and I stopped drawing. It looked like the one on our corner store. Afterward I told Ms. Rivera I felt like I'd been doing something important without knowing what it was called. She smiled. 'That's what tradition feels like. You recognize it because it was already in you.'

**Explore:** I'm reading about the Chicago and Philadelphia mural movements, my own cultural thread. I want to know whether anyone painted a mural in my neighborhood before ours.

**Show:** My sketchbook pages from the lecture. My reading list. A side-by-side of a slide from the lecture and our mural.

### Melu dote 6: Making, Visual Art and Craft

- **Persona:** Melu (First Act)
- **Category (primary lens):** Making
- **Subcategory:** Visual Art and Craft
- **Also seen through:** Excellence; Relationships
- **Sensitive topic:** none
- **Who can see it:** My group
- **Rep may use:** Yes

**Prompt:** What did you draw, paint, sculpt, sew or build by hand that matters to you?

**Do:** For the community center's open art show, I painted a large abstract canvas, full of bright interlocking figures, about my neighborhood's energy. I had two weeks, and I titled it 'Still Here.'

**Observe:** My planned sketches all failed; it only came together when I stopped planning and started painting. I named it 'Still Here' because that's what my community is. I learned that some paintings come from my head and some come from somewhere closer to my stomach.

**Tell:** I had two weeks and a stack of sketches I hated. One Saturday I gave up on the sketches, squeezed out warm orange, the color of sunset on my block, and started painting with no plan. Then came figures: dancers, runners, people reaching for each other. I kept layering until the canvas felt loud. At the show, people kept stopping in front of it. A woman told me it felt 'like coming home after a long day.' A little boy said it looked like a party. Both were right.

**Explore:** I'm entering 'Still Here' in the citywide youth competition, and it's going in my Ellington portfolio either way. I'm curious whether I can paint without a plan on purpose next time.

**Show:** The painting. The sketches that didn't work. The comment cards from the show.

### Melu dote 7: Wellbeing, Movement and Physical Activity

- **Persona:** Melu (First Act)
- **Category (primary lens):** Wellbeing
- **Subcategory:** Movement and Physical Activity
- **Also seen through:** Character
- **Sensitive topic:** none
- **Who can see it:** My group
- **Rep may use:** Yes

**Prompt:** What physical challenge did you work up to? How did you prepare?

**Do:** At summer dance camp, I learned a difficult West African-inspired contemporary routine. I struggled with it for four days, practiced every night, and performed it on day five.

**Observe:** For three days I was the worst dancer in the room, and I almost switched workshops. Practicing at night until my bunkmate made me stop is what changed it. I learned that muscle memory is a kind of trust.

**Tell:** The routine had sharp isolations and low groundwork that felt impossible. For three days I was the worst dancer in the room, and I was ready to switch to the drawing workshop. On day four, Miss Adjua stopped next to me. 'Your body is learning. Your brain needs to get out of the way.' That night I practiced in the cabin until my bunkmate threw a pillow at me. On day five the music started and my body took over. The transitions flowed. When the music stopped, the room erupted. Miss Adjua pointed at me. 'That is what persistence looks like.' I cried, the happy, exhausted kind.

**Explore:** I'm bringing the routine to my dance class and adding my own elements. I want to know whether I could perform it at my Ellington audition.

**Show:** The camp's video of the final performance, shared with the camp's permission. My practice notes.
